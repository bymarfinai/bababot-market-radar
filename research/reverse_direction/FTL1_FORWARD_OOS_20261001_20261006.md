## Why the selector can identify bad LONGs but still lose as SHORT

The selected forward population lost about $2.04 per trade historically as LONG.

Under the old historical runtime cost model of approximately 0.19% round-trip, this corresponds to roughly:

- historical LONG net: about -0.407% per $500 trade
- inferred raw signal-to-close directional move: about **-0.217%**

But by the time the 75s rule fires:
- median side return is already **-0.1485%**

So much of the original opposite-side move has already occurred before the SHORT entry.

The remaining raw downside is on the order of only several basis points for the average selected trade, while corrected SHORT round-trip friction is about:
- fee 0.10%
- modeled slippage 0.04%
- total ~**0.14%**

This resolves the apparent paradox:

> The rule is a good detector of “this LONG is bad,” but a poor detector of “there is still enough SHORT edge left from this point.”

These are different prediction targets.
